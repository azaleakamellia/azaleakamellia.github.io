Burning Incense in Fire-Repellant Coveralls: A Consultant's Guide to Inhumanity

Azalea Kamellia — April 2026

The reality check for experienced consultants that are being inappropriately labeled by clients: 'Some clients place auditors for your work to catch you in the act of not fulfilling what they require.' It's tough pill to swallow but I believe it is my civic duty to remind all the workforce on the face of the earth that if you measure the world by your 'unique' standards, it'll never fulfill your checklist. We are always meeting people half way based on the monetary investment you agreed and signed upon in good faith if legalities are to be brought afront. Should it be done in 'bad' faith with evidence of implicating the people you are collaborating with, then I believe, it goes both ways. When you work with humans who came from anything remotely close to your background, you tend to have a sense of belonging to the organization you represent and is compelled to bring forward attitudes as well as the mentality that will retain the organization's needs and wants first and foremost. Environmental consultancy is a lot like the service industry - the customer is always right. But you tend to become so clouded in your servitude that injustice in reciprocal details, inequality professional decorum as well as blatant one-sided complaints become your breakfast and heartburn become your best friend not to mention deteriorating mental health. I am not naive to not understand that there are two sides of a coin. Even so, regardless of any amount of HR intervention or management 'damage-control' meetings set for a more harmonious collaborative work between environmental consultancies and corporates, it won't ever fix any reputations damaged, caliber being questioned and moral values speed of continuous downfall. Rather, it accelerates it. For example, in my case, no amount of work from my end is going to be satisfactory when it is the relationship that has been set to a sick game of master and slave. My 10 years of experience working in the laboratory, on the field and in the office are downsized into mere burning incense when a single executive complained of my inability to withstand heatwave while fighting remnants of fever under direct 10.30 am sunlight at a coastal environment, in fire-repellant coveralls and safety boots, surveying plants with shared cameras that is being tossed between 4 teams scattered around with 3 + 1 walkie-talkies. Was I angry? Pissed, understandably. Was it unexpected? No. Unfortunately, I can't say I did not see it coming.

Sidenote: [I do try not to be too angry though...and it paid off. I barely feel any emotion when it comes to work these days. Having empathy is good. Misplaced ones makes you naive]

Work has been that way for me for the last 2 years. My usability has to be 80% and above although I am already on standby to provide any services necessary as well and to explain myself should it fall below 80%. If I do have any hours given to me that I planned to spread out appropriately to cover all the phases of my work within the duration of the project, I am forced to 'save' myself of this 80%-usability-hours on weekly basis by burning it up on the weeks I do not have the accumulation of 80% threshold out of 37.5 hours of total working hours. I joined the environmental consultancy with the specifics of providing my expertise to up the game of environmental resource management (pun not at all intended) with geospatial solutions with clear pathway of going technical for professional growth. At this juncture of my career, I am required to do business development which I am not opposed to. But expectation is neither here nor there. Why? Because my profession seems to give the impression that I am a certain software brand fanatics who coincidentally make maps and understand how environmental consultancies work just enough to be at the back and call of making maps; technical support. They give me work, I work. And right now, it is dangerous to not understand other people's work because it bleeds you dry. Sometimes, consultants aren't strong enough to defend their work that they are revising work over and over again, and before you know it, you have 7 versions of the same map and the latest 8th looks suspiciously the carbon copy of your first draft. Not to mention impossible deadlines, disregard for human resources required to complete a task beautifully or successfully and the essays for comments in agreed upon templates which revolves more towards grammatical or template policing rather than content development. I guess, there's always a first time in biodiversity baseline reporting anywhere for any corporate that wants to be at the right side of history when climate change dooms us all.

I believe, I am burned out. Not because I hate my job. I think, my experience working all over has let see the different kind of people we work with and there are many reasons for such thing to happen. It's not always business, people…let's get that crappy principle out of our thoughts. Men and women alike have prejudice and bias. And those are built upon experiences, personal and professional. You want to be able to make sound rational judgement, but even your way of thinking is based on your sentiment to remain impartial. It's ok to take offense, we are all entitled to it. Our profession is a mark of our identity, what we work so hard for. Humans aren't meant to live idly and aimless. With experience, comes ego. With ego, comes the allure of demanding respect under the guise of authoritative responsibility. In a simpler term, its mere authority capitalism based on differing educational achievements and organization's reputation. Based on how things are at this day and age, I am unfazed. How do you even stop someone from being proud of themselves especially if that's the only thing in their lives worth fighting for? You can't take that away from people who can't be a human being while working. It'll make them inhuman. What do you think?
