    Note 03: On the Epistemology of Satellite Precipitation Bias Correction
    Essay

    Reflecting on the limits of machine learning when ground-truth station data is sparse. What does it mean to "correct" a satellite estimate when our reference data is itself uncertain? This note explores the philosophical and practical tensions in bias correction methodologies, particularly in data-sparse tropical regions where rain gauge networks are inadequate.

    The fundamental relationship \( \text{SPI}_{\text{corrected}} = f(X_{\text{satellite}}, X_{\text{station}}, \theta_{\text{ML}}) \) assumes that \( X_{\text{station}} \) represents truth. But what happens when the station network itself introduces systematic errors? This essay questions whether we are truly correcting bias or merely transferring uncertainty from one domain to another.

    Note 02: The Scholar-Practitioner in the Age of Greenwashing
    Essay

    Why academic rigor and peer-reviewed quality must anchor corporate climate risk profiling. As ESG reporting becomes mandatory under TCFD and IFRS S2 frameworks, there is a risk that climate risk assessment becomes a box-ticking exercise rather than a genuine engagement with physical realities.

    This essay argues for the role of the "scholar-practitioner"—someone who maintains academic standards while engaging with commercial applications. It explores how principles of Sadaqah Jariyah (beneficial knowledge) and Tijarah & Ihsan (fair, excellent commerce) can guide ethical commercial practice in climate consulting.
